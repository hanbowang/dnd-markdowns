# Creating a Background

A character background helps connect a player character to the world, the campaign, and the party. For a DM, backgrounds are useful tools for building personal hooks, NPC connections, and meaningful consequences.

## Purpose of a Background

A good background should answer:

- Where did the character come from?
- What shaped their beliefs?
- Who matters to them?
- What problems followed them into the adventure?
- Why are they willing to risk their life?

## Core Elements

### Origin

Define where the character is from. This can be a village, city, noble estate, monastery, battlefield, criminal hideout, or wilderness region.

Consider:

- Was the place safe or dangerous?
- Is the character welcome there?
- What local customs shaped them?
- Does the place still exist?

### Upbringing

Describe the conditions that shaped the character’s early life.

Examples:

- Raised by a trade guild
- Trained in a temple
- Orphaned during a war
- Born into nobility
- Grew up among smugglers
- Lived as a wanderer

### Defining Event

Give the character one important event that changed their life.

Examples:

- They witnessed a betrayal.
- They survived a monster attack.
- They were falsely accused of a crime.
- They discovered forbidden knowledge.
- They lost someone important.
- They were chosen by a mysterious patron.

### Bonds

Create people, places, or groups the character cares about.

Useful bonds include:

- A family member
- A mentor
- A rival
- A lost friend
- A hometown
- A faction
- A former adventuring companion

### Flaws and Secrets

A background becomes more useful when it includes complications.

Possible flaws:

- Owes a dangerous debt
- Hides their true identity
- Fears abandonment
- Distrusts authority
- Is haunted by guilt
- Has a powerful enemy

Possible secrets:

- They are related to someone important.
- They know the location of a hidden place.
- They once served the villain.
- They carry an heirloom with unknown meaning.
- They were spared by a monster or enemy for unknown reasons.

## Questions for Players

Ask the player a few focused questions:

1. Who taught you the most important lesson of your life?
2. What place do you never want to return to?
3. Who would be happy to see you alive?
4. Who would be angry to see you alive?
5. What rumor might people have heard about you?
6. What do you want that adventuring might help you obtain?

## DM Usage

Use backgrounds to create:

- Personal quests
- Returning NPCs
- Moral dilemmas
- Faction relationships
- Clues tied to the character’s past
- Emotional stakes during major story arcs

Avoid using a background only to punish the character. A good background should create both opportunities and complications.

## Quick Background Template

Use this structure for fast character creation:

```text
I came from [place].
I was shaped by [person, group, or hardship].
My life changed when [defining event].
I care about [bond].
I am afraid of or troubled by [flaw, secret, or unresolved problem].
I adventure because [motivation].
```

## Example

```text
I came from a fishing village on the edge of a haunted coast.
I was shaped by my grandmother, who taught me old stories about sea spirits.
My life changed when a storm destroyed half the village and left strange symbols carved into the docks.
I care about my younger brother, who vanished after following the symbols inland.
I am troubled by the belief that the storm was meant for me.
I adventure because I need to find my brother and learn why the sea has marked my family.
```
